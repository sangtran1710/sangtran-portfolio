Destruction work for real-time projects isn't just about blowing meshes apart—it's about turning expensive physics simulations into lightweight, runtime-ready data.

I used Houdini mainly to move heavy offline simulation into reusable textures, atlases, and pre-fractured assets.

## 1. Multi-Stage RBD & Forcefield Collapse

<VideoPlayer
  src="/assets/blog/houdini-wolverine-destructibles/wolverine-houdini-rbd-bridge.mp4"
  poster="/assets/blog/houdini-wolverine-destructibles/poster-rbd-bridge.webp"
  title="Bridge Destruction & Forcefield Collapse"
  caption="Bullet solver setup handling progressive structural failure across a suspension bridge span."
/>

Large structures look unnatural when pieces shatter all at once. I broke the collapse into sequential phases:

- **Constraint hierarchy**: Primary framing held by stiff `Glue` constraints, which switch to `Soft` and `ConeTwist` as stress builds up to allow plastic bending before release.
- **Directional forcefield**: Rather than passive collisions, an animated directional velocity volume pushes through the structure to drive failure along realistic load lines.
- **Debris separation**: High-frequency surface concrete shards are split from structural core chunks for downstream LOD and culling.

## 2. Modular Prop Destruction & Clustering

<VideoPlayer
  src="/assets/blog/houdini-wolverine-destructibles/wolverine-houdini-prop-destruction.mp4"
  poster="/assets/blog/houdini-wolverine-destructibles/poster-prop-destruction.webp"
  title="Prop Fracture & Exploded View Validation"
  caption="Exploded view showing interior structural ribs, metal panel separation, and collision piece clusters."
/>

Level designers placed dozens of industrial props across environments—rooftop HVAC units, electrical boxes, ducts. Manually cutting these by hand was impractical, so I built an HDA pipeline (`dtk_fracture` + `dtk_validator`) to automate the pass.

### What I automated:
- **Interior vs. exterior cuts**: Automatic vertex color and material ID assignment between pristine outer metal and interior concrete/metal cut faces.
- **Cluster grouping**: Clumping tiny Voronoi shards into larger physical clusters to keep rigid body counts within real-time physics budgets.
- **Topology validation**: `dtk_validator` flags non-manifold geometry, flipped normals, and concavity before geometry ever leaves Houdini.

## 3. Ground Impact Cracks & COP Vector Displacement Baker

Combat impacts need physical bite on the floor. Standard heightmaps only push vertices along a single normal vector, which can't produce lateral tearing, undercut rock edges, or jagged trench lips.

For ground strikes, I authored procedural crack growth networks in Houdini that bake full **3D Vector Displacement Maps (VDM)** and tangent normal maps through **COP (Compositing Operators)** networks.

## 4. 3D Volume Slicing to Texture Sheet

High-resolution volumetric simulations are generally too expensive to reproduce directly at runtime at the same fidelity.

To bring dense smoke and shockwave plumes into the project without simulating 3D fluid grids dynamically, I set up a slicing pipeline (`slice_vdb`):

<VideoPlayer
  src="/assets/blog/houdini-wolverine-destructibles/wolverine-houdini-volume-texture.mp4"
  poster="/assets/blog/houdini-wolverine-destructibles/poster-volume-texture.webp"
  title="OpenVDB 3D Volume Slicing"
  caption="Slicing high-resolution volumetric smoke simulations into contiguous grid sheets for real-time raymarching."
/>

1. **Field clamp & normalize**: Normalize VDB density and temperature to $[0, 1]$ bounds.
2. **Equidistant slicing**: Slice the 3D voxel volume into discrete planar cross-sections (50 Z-slices).
3. **Atlas packing**: Pack cross-sections into a single 4096&times;4096 power-of-two texture sheet.
4. **Shader raymarch**: At runtime, a lightweight custom shader steps through the slice coordinates to reconstruct volumetric depth without requiring runtime 3D fluid simulation.

---

The core goal across these setups was always the same: keep the artist control and simulation fidelity of Houdini, but package the output into clean, highly optimized data—compact textures, clustered shards, and lightweight atlases—that runtime shaders and collision systems can consume within production frame budgets.
