## Mesh and vertex masks

The **Fall** mesh spans three continuous zones—the shallow approach leading to the lip, the curved rollover, and the vertical falling sheet—so flow UVs stay uninterrupted across the bend. In Houdini, I authored vertex color attributes directly on the geometry to drive shader behavior per region:

- **R = Edge** (border fade and containment)
- **G = Foam** (localized foam intensity)
- **B = Motion** (flow weighting across the rollover and drop)
- **Alpha** (separates the upper approach from the falling sheet)

Primary UVs follow the flow direction, while a secondary UV channel stores speed data (`uv2.y`) for scrolling rates. Both the Fall and Channel branches are exported as separate meshes sharing the exact same pivot (`MODELS_BOUNDS`) so they align cleanly when assembled.

## Procedural textures authored in Houdini COP2

Both materials rely on packed 1024 × 2048 procedural textures authored in Houdini COP2 so multiple surface signals can be sampled from a single texture fetch.

### Channel Crest Relief

The **Channel Crest Relief** graph packs three scalar maps into RGB:

- **R = Wave height** — drives surface displacement along the shallow flow.
- **G = Crest foam** — primary foam mask concentrated around wave peaks.
- **B = Crest foam variation** — secondary foam pattern blended against `G` over time so foam breaks up and evolves within the same crest region rather than sliding as a static decal.

### Fall Whitewater

The **Fall Whitewater** graph combines fine streaks, foam clusters, and vertical bands before packing them with V-seam wrapping and soft fades on both edges:

- **R = Whitewater coverage** — primary streak and foam opacity across the sheet.
- **G = Relief** — high-frequency surface detail aligned to the whitewater streaks.
- **B = Broad displacement** — low-frequency deformation across the falling curtain.

## Material integration and scope

At runtime, the vertex masks (`Edge`, `Foam`, `Motion`, `Alpha`) and flow/speed UVs control where and how fast the packed COP2 textures scroll, displace, and blend across the rollover. This study focuses strictly on mesh topology, vertex data, procedural COP2 textures, and isolated shader motion; secondary splash/mist emitters, FLIP simulations, and full-scene profiling are outside the scope of these previews.
