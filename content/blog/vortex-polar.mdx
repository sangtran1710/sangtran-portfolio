Most texture panning shaders move coordinates in straight directions along the horizontal (U) and vertical (V) Cartesian axes. But if you want to create a black hole, a portal, a swirling vortex, or a magical circle, scrolling straight textures will never look right.

To scroll textures circularly, we must convert our coordinates from **Cartesian (X, Y)** space to **Polar (Radius, Angle)** space.

---

## The Concept: Cartesian vs. Polar


- **Cartesian UVs:** Every point is defined by a horizontal distance `u` and vertical distance `v`. Scrolling UVs moves textures in straight lines.
- **Polar UVs:** Every point is defined by:
  1. A distance from the center (Radius r).
  2. An angle around the center (Angle theta or phi).
  
If we map a flat texture to Polar coordinates, scrolling the horizontal coordinate (u = Radius) will cause the texture to flow **inward/outward** from the center. Scrolling the vertical coordinate (v = Angle) will cause the texture to **rotate** around the center.

## Mathematical Conversion

To convert a flat Cartesian coordinate system to Polar, we center our UVs by subtracting `0.5` so the center of rotation is `(0, 0)`. Then:

1. **Radius (r):** The Pythagorean theorem calculates distance from the center:
   `r = sqrt(x^2 + y^2)`
2. **Angle (phi):** The trigonometric function calculates the angle in radians:
   `phi = atan2(y, x)`

---

## Custom HLSL Implementation

Here is the HLSL code to center Cartesian coordinates, calculate polar space, apply a spiral swirl offset, and return the final distorted coordinates:

```hlsl
// Convert Cartesian UVs to Polar space
float2 centeredUv = uv - 0.5;
float r = length(centeredUv);
float phi = atan2(centeredUv.y, centeredUv.x);

// Apply spiral twist distortion over time
// Scrolling X moves texture inward. Scrolling Y rotates the texture.
float u = r * tiling.x + time * speed.x;
float v = (phi / 6.2831853) * tiling.y + r * swirlAmount + time * speed.y;

float2 polarUv = float2(u, v);
```

## Step-by-Step Shader Setup

### Step 1: UV Remap
Connect your mesh UVs to the polar converter code block. If you look at the output of the conversion node, a noise texture will look like a swirling galaxy.

### Step 2: The "Center Pinch" Fix
Because polar coordinate math squishes the entire width of the texture into the absolute center point `(0,0)`, you will notice an ugly, pixelated pinch artifact right in the center.

To fix this:
1. Create a radial gradient mask using distance from the center.
2. Invert the gradient and multiply it by your texture opacity.
3. This fades the texture to 0 opacity at the exact center point, hiding the ugly pinch.

---

## Common Production Traps & Solutions

### 1. High Tiling Aliasing
**Why it happens:** Because the texture repeats around the circle, when it scrolls near the center, the pixels get packed extremely close together, causing heavy aliasing and moiré patterns.
**How to fix it:** Use Mipmapping, or fade out high-frequency texture details as the radius approaches zero.

### 2. Mesh Layout & Circular Planes
**Why it happens:** Applying a polar shader on a square plane wastes a lot of pixel cost on transparent corners where opacity is 0.
**How to fix it:** Ditch square planes. Use a circular disc mesh with 12-16 sides. This saves fill-rate performance on the GPU by clipping off transparent waste zones.
