This is a personal real-time material study built in Unreal Engine 5, exploring how to author a reactive Sci-Fi / Biohazard terminal shader entirely through procedural math in the material graph—without relying on external flipbook textures or pre-rendered video clips.

The goal was to build a flexible, unlit masked shader that combines organic emissive breathing, fine CRT scanlines, and timed procedural glitch slices, with all critical behaviors exposed as clean parameters for technical artists and level designers.

## 1. Deconstructing the Glitch & Scanline Layers

A common pitfall with digital glitch shaders is applying uniform screen noise, which looks like camera static rather than a malfunctioning CRT terminal. To make the distortion feel mechanical and localized, I split the effect into discrete mathematical passes:

1. **Quantized UV Slices**: Instead of a smooth distortion, UV coordinates are quantized into horizontal bands using `Floor` and step divisions. Each band samples a pseudo-random jitter offset, causing distinct horizontal rows of the logo to tear left or right.
2. **Periodic Emissive Breath**: A low-frequency sine wave smoothly scales brightness between `BreathMin` and 1.0, giving the warning sign a living, rhythmic pulse.
3. **CRT Scanlines**: A high-frequency sine function driven by UV coordinates produces evenly spaced scanlines. Artists can adjust `ScanlineCount` (e.g. 220) and `ScanlineStrength` to match anything from subtle modern holographic UI to harsh retro phosphor monitors.
4. **Burst Timing & Glitch Force**: A timer network controls glitch bursts, exposing `BurstInterval`, `GlitchDuration`, and `BurstChance`. The `GlitchForce` slider allows instant manual override for cutscene triggers or cinematic gameplay events.

## 2. Material Graph Architecture (172 Nodes, 27 Parameters)

To keep a complex 172-node shader maintainable and readable across a team, I structured the graph into distinct functional blocks:

![Overview of the modular color-coded material graph in Unreal Engine 5](/assets/blog/ue5-terminal-shader/material-graph-overview.webp)

- **[1] Glitch Run (Red)**: Handles the primary UV step divisions and directional slice offsets along the horizontal axis.
- **[2] Glitch Noise (Yellow)**: Computes high-frequency bandline noise, jitter thresholds, and random dropouts.
- **[3] Logo Sample & Island UV (Blue)**: Manages logo texture sampling and coordinates across isolated graphic islands.
- **[4] Island Flicker (Purple)**: Applies per-band dropout factors and intermittent electrical flicker to individual components of the symbol.
- **[5] Breath & Scanlines (Green)**: Generates the base breathing pulse and CRT line frequency.
- **[6] Composite (Blue)**: Blends the glow falloff, emissive intensity, warning tint (`WarningColor`), and backdrop opacity into the final Emissive and Opacity Mask pins.

## 3. Real-Time Performance & Pipeline Benefits

Authoring this effect as a procedural shader rather than a flipbook or media player offers distinct production advantages:

- **Zero Texture Streaming Overhead**: Because all distortion, scanlines, and breathing pulses are evaluated procedurally via lightweight math nodes (`Sin`, `Frac`, `Step`, `Clamp`), there is zero texture memory footprint for animation frames.
- **Resolution Independence**: The graphic and scanlines remain razor-sharp at any viewing distance or camera orbit angle, scaling seamlessly from small handheld props to giant holographic billboards.
- **Instant Iteration via Instances**: Exposing 27 parameters in `MI_Biohazard_Terminal` means artists can change warning colors, switch from slow standby pulses to frantic alarms, or trigger scripted damage without touching shader code or re-exporting textures.
