When fractured props come out of physics simulations, they are usually dense, multi-material meshes that take a lot of repetitive cleanup before they are usable for VFX. You have to locate the fracture material, isolate internal break surfaces, strip out the untouched outer hull, generate lightweight emitter cards, and make sure UV directions align for scrolling textures.

Doing that by hand across dozens of assets is slow and error-prone, so I wrote this Blender Python operator to handle the whole cleanup and prep pass in a few clicks.

---

## Isolating fracture surfaces

The tool scans the selected mesh and lists its assigned materials directly in the operator panel. Selecting the internal crack material isolates those faces and purges exterior shell geometry.

![Fracture material preview in Blender](/assets/blog/destructible-separate-mesh-tool/destructible-material-selected.webp)

Once split, only the internal break geometry remains, cleaned of zero-area faces and ready for emitter generation or mesh extraction.

![Separated crack mesh ready for cleanup](/assets/blog/destructible-separate-mesh-tool/separated-crack-mesh.webp)

---

## Generating lightweight emitter meshes

Feeding dense fracture geometry into a runtime particle system is wasteful. 

Instead, the tool breaks the isolated fracture faces into loose islands, calculates their center points, filters out points that sit within a minimum proximity threshold, and generates minimal single-triangle emitter cards at those coordinates. This gives particle systems an accurate spawn volume without pushing thousands of unnecessary vertices into the engine.

![Generated VFX emitter from separated cracks](/assets/blog/destructible-separate-mesh-tool/vfx-emitter-generated.webp)

---

## Aligning UV flow for erosion shaders

Dissolve edges, crack glows, and erosion shaders depend on consistent UV orientation. Manually unwrapping dozens of loose shards usually produces mismatched flow vectors where textures scroll against the grain of the crack.

The tool inspects shard topology and assigns an appropriate layout:
- **Face Grid** for flat emitter cards, packing them into normalized coordinate space.
- **Flow & Caps** for elongated shards, unwrapping along their length so panning noise follows the fracture path naturally.
- **Straight Strip** for continuous ribbon-like cracks.

![Noise flow preview for FX UV validation](/assets/blog/destructible-separate-mesh-tool/noise-flow-preview.webp)

A procedural noise preview in the viewport lets me verify that flow directions and dissolve gradients read correctly before exporting FBX meshes into the runtime pipeline.
